Skip the 3D tour

A camera in the brim of a baseball cap that estimates the calories and macros in my dining-hall meals. It photographs the tray, a Telegram bot names each item and estimates the portion, and the numbers come from UMD Dining's published nutrition facts.

It started as camera glasses, but the battery was too big to hide in a frame, so I moved it to a cap and took on the software too. The hardware only has to take a good photo of the tray, and everything else, from naming the food to adding up the macros, happens on the Telegram side.

The cap, exploded in 3D

Exploded view, not to scale: the small parts are drawn larger as they come out. Placement follows the build; sizes are approximate.

  1. 01 / 04

    The cap

    Everything rides in a baseball cap: the battery, the controls, the camera and its antenna.

  2. 02 / 04

    Apart

    The 500 mAh LiPo is a protected cell, tucked under the crown where it cannot get crushed. The touch pad is on the side of the crown and the slide switch is on the band.

  3. 03 / 04

    Under the brim

    The XIAO ESP32S3 Sense sits under the front of the brim, camera angled down at the tray. The Wi-Fi antenna faces away from my head and the recording LED is under the brim tip. The mic stays off, because Maryland requires everyone on an audio recording to consent.

  4. 04 / 04

    The shot

    It photographs the tray. A Telegram bot names each item and estimates the portion, and the numbers come from UMD Dining's published nutrition facts.
