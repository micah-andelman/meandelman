ENES100 OTV

Class
ENES100, Introduction to Engineering Design
When
Fall 2026
Role
Electrical and software lead on an eight-person team
Built with
Arduino UNO R4 WiFi, C++, arduino-cli, GitHub Actions

An autonomous vehicle for ENES100's water-sampling mission. An overhead camera tells it where it is in the arena. At the pool it lowers an arm, measures the water's depth, checks it for pollution and pumps a sample. Then it follows the wall past the obstacles and drives under the limbo to the goal.

I'm the only electrical engineer on the team. I wire the robot and write most of its code. My teammates each write a subsystem as its own Arduino sketch, and I merge those into one codebase and fix whatever breaks. I test everything in ENES100's browser simulator first. A single build flag switches the same code between the simulator and the real robot.

A personal agent I text on Telegram. It's Claude Code running headless on a server, with tools I wrote for my calendar, email, Canvas, grades and the campus buses. It messages me every morning and evening, and sends a longer review on Sundays.

Most of my time went into the tools. Each one does one job and is careful about what it doesn't know. If it guessed an exam date, it says so. If it can't find a room, it says that too. It only suggests a bus when the bus beats walking by at least five minutes.

Brimcam

When
Fall 2026
Role
Solo, hardware and software
Built with
Seeed XIAO ESP32S3 Sense, Herald, Gemini API

A camera in the brim of a baseball cap that estimates the calories and macros in my dining-hall meals. It photographs the tray, a Telegram bot names each item and estimates the portion, and the numbers come from UMD Dining's published nutrition facts.

It started as camera glasses, but the battery was too big to hide in a frame, so I moved it to a cap and took on the software too. The cap does the seeing and the bot does the thinking: the hardware only has to take a good photo of the tray, and everything else happens on the Telegram side.
