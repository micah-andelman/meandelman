ENES100 OTV

Class
ENES100, Introduction to Engineering Design
When
Fall 2026
Role
Electrical and software lead on an eight-person team
Built with
Arduino UNO R4 WiFi, C++, arduino-cli, GitHub Actions

An autonomous vehicle for ENES100's water-sampling mission. An overhead camera tells it where it is in the arena. At the pool it lowers an arm, measures the water's depth, checks it for pollution and pumps a sample. Then it follows the wall past the obstacles and drives under the limbo to the goal.

I'm the only electrical engineer on the team. I wire the robot and write most of its code. My teammates each write a subsystem as its own Arduino sketch, and I merge those into one codebase and fix whatever breaks. I test everything in ENES100's browser simulator first. A single build flag switches the same code between the simulator and the real robot.

The OTV in 3D

From the team's Onshape CAD. Arduino, breadboard and wiring added from photos.

  1. 01 / 06

    The robot

    A plywood deck on two drive wheels and a caster, 21.7 cm wide, 24.1 cm long with the arm down and 12.8 cm tall. It has to stay under 3 kg and fit under the limbo.

  2. 02 / 06

    Drive

    Two JGY370 worm-gear motors, 6 V and 30 RPM, each turning an 8 cm wheel directly, with a caster at the back. Running the sides at different speeds steers it and lets it turn in place.

  3. 03 / 06

    Brains

    An Arduino UNO R4 WiFi. The overhead vision system tracks the ArUco marker on top and sends position and heading over Wi-Fi. It has to stop within 150 mm of the pool.

  4. 04 / 06

    Arm

    A printed arm between two SG90 servos. One motion swings it down over the pool, so the sensors at the tip face the water and the intake tube dips in.

  5. 05 / 06

    Depth

    The HC-SR04 at the tip times a ping off the water to measure how deep it is. The reading has to be right within 4 mm.

  6. 06 / 06

    Sample

    A peristaltic pump, switched by a MOSFET on D10, pulls at least 20 mL up the tube. The sample has to stay on board until the end of the run.

Brimcam

When
Fall 2026
Role
Solo, hardware and software
Built with
Seeed XIAO ESP32S3 Sense, Herald, Gemini API

A camera in the brim of a baseball cap that estimates the calories and macros in my dining-hall meals. It photographs the tray, a Telegram bot names each item and estimates the portion, and the numbers come from UMD Dining's published nutrition facts.

It started as camera glasses, but the battery was too big to hide in a frame, so I moved it to a cap and took on the software too. The cap does the seeing and the bot does the thinking: the hardware only has to take a good photo of the tray, and everything else happens on the Telegram side.

  1. Crown. The battery hides underneath it.
  2. 500 mAh LiPo. A protected cell, tucked under the crown where it can't get crushed.
  3. Touch pad. On the side of the crown.
  4. Slide switch. On the band.
  5. Brim. The camera board sits under its front edge.
  6. XIAO ESP32S3 Sense. Camera and Wi-Fi, angled down at the tray. The onboard mic stays off, because Maryland requires everyone on an audio recording to consent.
  7. Wi-Fi antenna. Along the brim, facing away from my head.
  8. Recording LED. Under the brim tip.
